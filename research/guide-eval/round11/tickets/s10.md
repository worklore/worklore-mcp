Our Flutter app using `posthog_flutter` 5.x used up its free monthly PostHog event quota within a few days. PostHog charged by event count, and our own callbacks generated far more events than we expected.

The product spec called for events such as “user started typing their email” and “user moved the loan amount slider,” so we initially captured them in `onChanged`. That fired on every keystroke for text fields and every step crossed while dragging a slider. An email address became twenty events, and one slider drag became dozens. That multiplied across every field on every registration screen and every user. Looking at the PostHog dashboard didn’t expose the problem: the event names matched the spec and the funnels looked right, but there were far too many captures.

I kept every event name and property unchanged and changed only when the events fired, which preserved the existing dashboards and funnels. For text fields, I made the event fire on the first edit, once per screen visit, using a screen-state flag such as `bool _emailInputFired`. The general pattern was `bool _<field>InputFired = false;`, with capture guarded by `if (!_<field>InputFired)` and the flag set after capture. I also found a lifecycle trap: when the state object survived navigation between steps, the flag belonged in that surviving state, and “once” needed to mean either per visit or per flow.

For tap/focus events, I guarded capture in a `FocusNode` listener with `hasFocus && !_tapFired`. For events that needed the field’s final value, I moved capture from `onChanged` to the focus-lost branch, `!hasFocus`. That final-value property was limited to something non-sensitive, such as “is valid.”

For sliders, I moved capture from `onChanged` to `onChangeEnd`, so it fired once per drag when the user let go. I kept `onChanged` for UI updates only. For scrolls, I used `NotificationListener<ScrollEndNotification>` instead of capturing every scroll update through `ScrollUpdateNotification` or a `ScrollController` listener.

The same overcapture trap extended beyond direct `onChanged` calls: `Posthog().capture`, our capture wrapper, or an analytics `log` call could also run through a method called by the callback, a `TextEditingController` / `ScrollController` listener, or a scroll-update handler. Without a once-only flag or debounce in front of those calls, they captured each change too.
